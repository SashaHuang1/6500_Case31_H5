IC-Case 31: Hypothesis Testing (H5a-H5d)

Employees Federal Credit Union | Multiple Regression

1. Introduction

The Employees Federal Credit Union (EFCU) case investigates members’ views of credit union services and operations. This report examines whether confidentiality, prompt loan processing, adequacy of services, and loan application simplicity predict members’ overall evaluations of EFCU management and operations. A multiple regression evaluates all four predictors simultaneously.

2. Research Hypotheses

  • H5a: Belief that EFCU keeps personal financial information confidential positively influences overall attitudes toward operational efficiency (Q31).
  • H5b: Perceived promptness of loan application processing positively influences overall attitudes toward operational efficiency (Q32).
  • H5c: Perceived adequacy of financial services in meeting member needs positively influences overall attitudes toward operational efficiency (Q33).
  • H5d: Perceived simplicity and ease of loan applications positively influences overall attitudes toward operational efficiency (Q34).

The dependent variable is Q37_r, the recoded overall evaluation of EFCU management and operations. The original Q37 scale codes excellent = 1 through very poor = 5; the recoded variable must be verified to ensure that higher values represent more positive evaluations. Q31-Q34 use 1 = strongly disagree through 5 = strongly agree.

5. Hypothesis Tests and Interpretation

A directional hypothesis is supported at the 5% significance level only if the estimated coefficient is positive and its p-value is below 0.05, assuming higher Q37_r means a more positive evaluation. The coefficients describe conditional associations; the survey design does not establish causation.

labels <- c(Q31 = "H5a: Confidentiality", Q32 = "H5b: Processing promptness",
            Q33 = "H5c: Service adequacy", Q34 = "H5d: Application simplicity")
for (v in names(labels)) {
  b <- coef(model)[v]
  p <- coef(summary(model))[v, "Pr(>|t|)"]
  result <- if (is.na(p)) "Not estimable" else if (b > 0 && p < .05) "Supported" else "Not supported"
  cat(sprintf("%s: b = %.3f, p = %.4f. %s.\n\n", labels[v], b, p, result))
}

Because the measures are single survey items, interpretations should consider measurement error and common-method bias. Missing observations are excluded listwise. Inspect residual patterns, influential observations, and potential multicollinearity before relying on the model. Coefficients should not be interpreted as causal effects.
